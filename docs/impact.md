# Impact and Evidence

## Who has the problem

Conference planning teams: university conferences (Yale Business & the Environment, Yale Philanthropy), nonprofit and professional summits (Urban Tree Summit), and the many small teams that run them, often students or staff squeezing it in around other work. Alex has planned conferences professionally (Casey Trees / Urban Tree Summit) and is on the planning team for a Yale environmental conference. **Finding good speakers is one of the hardest, slowest parts.**

## How people cope today

| Option | What it does | What's missing |
|---|---|---|
| Google / LinkedIn searching | Finds names one at a time | Hours of tabs; no check that the role is current; no sense of whether they speak; no cross-sector balance |
| Speaker bureaus (BigSpeak, Leading Authorities, AllAmericanSpeakers) | Curated rosters of professional speakers | Expensive, celebrity-heavy, few practitioners or regional experts, little for niche topics like urban forestry |
| Conference speaker lists (Trellis, Climate Week, REGENERATE) | Shows who has spoken | Scattered across sites; not searchable by your theme |
| ChatGPT / general chatbots | Fast name suggestions | **Can invent people, titles and links;** no source checking; no shortlist, panels or outreach tracking |
| Spreadsheets for outreach | Tracking | Manual; disconnected from the research |

**What's new in Speaker Scout:** cross-sector discovery (6 kinds of speakers at once) **with every person verified against real pages in code**, speaking history surfaced, a Panel Architect that designs and critiques cross-discipline panels, and the planning workflow (conferences, panels, outreach status, follow-ups, drafted emails, chat research notes) in one place.

## Time and cost (sourced estimates; measured where possible)

- **Speaker Scout:** about **1.5 minutes and about $0.03** (tokens) for about **15 verified speakers across 6 sectors**, each with a profile link, speaking history and topic tags (measured, official runs).
- **Manual research:** finding, checking and writing up one speaker typically takes about 15–30 minutes (search, open the profile, confirm the role is current, look for past talks). For 15 speakers that's **about 4–7 hours** of a planner's time. *(Alex's estimate from planning the Urban Tree Summit at Casey Trees, confirmed Oct 26.)*
- **That's roughly 160–280× faster.** The planner's time goes into judgment and outreach instead of tab-hunting.

## Limits

- Results depend on what's public online. Early-career and community voices with little web presence are harder to verify, which is why Advocates is sometimes thin.
- Searches take 1–2.5 minutes because every person is verified.
- The app suggests and organizes. A human always decides and sends the invitations.

## Real user feedback: YBEC speaker outreach lead

Leah, a second-year student who leads speaker outreach for the Yale Business & the Environment Conference, tried Speaker Scout. Her feedback, as relayed by Alex (paraphrased):

- The site is awesome and would be a huge help to the conference.
- Researching good speakers is the hardest part, and it takes a long time just to get started.
- It's a chicken-and-egg problem: you don't know who the speakers are, so you don't know what panel themes to make, and without themes it's hard to know who to reach out to.
- Having names and bios pulled to start from is a huge help.

What this shows: the problem is confirmed by the person who does the job, plus a benefit not framed before. Speaker Scout breaks the chicken-and-egg loop by surfacing real, verified people first and letting the Panel Architect propose themes around them.
