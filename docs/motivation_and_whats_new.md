# Motivation and What Is New (research for report sections 2–3)

Researched Oct 26, 2026. Sources are linked inline. **Caution:** many speaker-industry statistics come from speaker bureaus, which sell booking services; they are labeled as such.

## 2. Motivation

### Who has this problem?

- **Conference and event planning teams**, especially small ones: university conferences run by students and staff (Yale Business & the Environment, Yale Philanthropy), nonprofit and professional summits (Casey Trees' Urban Tree Summit), associations, and corporate event teams. Alex has planned conferences professionally and is on a Yale conference planning team now.
- **It's a big, stressful job.** "Event coordinator" has ranked around **#5 on CareerCast's "Most Stressful Jobs"** list for years because of deadlines, public scrutiny and juggling many moving parts ([Smart Meetings](https://smartmeetings.com/?p=63381), [Meetings & Conventions](https://www.meetings-conventions.com/News/Meetings-Events/Stress-in-Our-Ranks), [Eventflare](https://eventflare.io/journal/event-coordinator-why-its-one-of-the-most-stressful-jobs-around)).

### How do they deal with it today?

1. **Word of mouth.** A PCMA Convene survey found that for **70% of planners, keynoters are found through word of mouth** ([PCMA Convene](https://www.pcmaconvene.org/features/cmp-series/are-you-finding-the-best-speakers-for-your-events/)). That means the same well-connected people get asked again and again, which is a known cause of repetitive, non-diverse lineups ([Innovation Women](https://innovationwomen.com/the-whys-of-the-all-male-panel/)).
2. **Manual Googling and LinkedIn:** one name at a time. Open each profile, check the role is current, hunt for past talks, then copy everything into a spreadsheet. A speakers bureau lists the steps of DIY sourcing as *research, vetting, negotiating, coordinating logistics, legal and accounting* ([AAE via search summary](https://www.allamericanspeakers.com/blog/how-to-book-the-right-speaker-for-your-event/), [EventsAir guide](https://www.eventsair.com/blog/how-to-find-speakers-for-an-event)).
3. **Speaker bureaus:** curated rosters of professional speakers. **Average national keynote fees are about $10,000, starting around $7,500** ([SPEAKING.com](https://speaking.com/speakers-bureau-services-faqs)), and bureaus typically take **20–30% commission** ([Futurist Speakers](https://www.futuristsspeakers.com/what-percentage-do-speakers-bureaus-take/)). Their rosters lean toward professional and celebrity speakers, not the practitioners, city officials and researchers a niche conference needs.
4. **Speaker marketplaces and calls for speakers** (SpeakerHub, eSpeakers, SpeakerMatch, Sessionize): these show only people who signed up or applied.
5. **ChatGPT and other chatbots:** fast name suggestions, but **unreliable**. Studies of ChatGPT-generated references found **47% fabricated** in a medical study of 115 references ([PMC10484980](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10484980)), and **55% (GPT-3.5) and 18% (GPT-4) fabricated** across 636 citations ([tech.co summary](https://tech.co/news/chatgpt-fabricates-references-half-the-time)). Fabricated entries often used *real-sounding names with plausible titles*, which is exactly the risk when asking a chatbot for "speakers on X."
6. **Spreadsheets, email and shared docs** for panels, outreach and follow-ups, disconnected from the research.

### What does it cost them?

- **Time:** finding, vetting and writing up one speaker typically takes about **15–30 minutes**, so **4–7 hours for 15 speakers** (Alex's estimate from conference work; to be confirmed with a teammate). Speaker Scout did it in about **1.5 minutes for about 15 verified speakers** (official runs).
- **Money:** bureau-sourced keynotes run about **$7.5k–$10k+ with 20–30% commission** built in. A bad fit wastes that and the audience's time.
- **Frustration and risk:** in a 2026 client survey by a speakers bureau, **72% of planners named finding a speaker who fits their audience as a major hurdle**, and **72% named staying within budget**; about 28% said evaluating speaker quality before booking is a major pain point ([Speakers Spotlight, 2026, bureau survey](https://www.speakers.ca/2026/07/the-biggest-challenges-event-planners-face-when-booking-a-speaker-and-how-to-solve-them/)).
- **Lineup quality:** relying on the same circuit produces homogeneous lineups. Across ~60,000 speakers at professional events, **about 69% were men** ([Bizzabo via NPR](https://www.npr.org/2018/11/01/663012390/survey-suggests-manels-all-male-panels-are-still-the-norm)); a JAMA Network Open study of 98 medical conferences found **women were about 30% of speakers and over a third of panels were all-male** ([Scimex](https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/manels-still-rife-at-medical-conferences-with-over-a-third-of-panels-only-featuring-men)). A common organizer excuse is "we couldn't find anyone" ([Monday-8am](https://www.monday-8am.com/say-no-to-manels/)).

### Why should anyone care?

Speakers *are* the conference: they're why people register and what attendees remember. Small, mission-driven conferences (universities, nonprofits, community summits) can't afford bureaus and don't have time for hours of research, so they fall back on whoever they already know. A tool that **widens the pool across sectors**, **proves each person is real and current**, and **keeps the panel and outreach work organized** helps these teams build better, more varied programs in a fraction of the time.

## 3. What already exists, and what's different

| Existing option | What it does well | What it doesn't do |
|---|---|---|
| **ChatGPT / Claude / Perplexity** (general AI chat, incl. deep research) | Fast brainstorming; can browse and cite | No guarantee people or links are real (studies above); no structured categories; no saved shortlist, panels, outreach tracking or team workflow; results vary run to run |
| **[Lessie AI Conference Speaker Finder](https://lessie.ai/conference-speaker-finder)** | Plain-English brief → shortlist with "last spoken at" and source URLs; **finds and validates email addresses**; drafts invites | Built around **harvesting contact emails**, a privacy trade-off; no code-level check of claims; no panel design or review; no outreach tracking (per its page) |
| **[SpeakUp](https://speak-up.pro/en)** (founded 2025) | AI ranks **70,000+ registered speaker profiles** against a brief; messaging and booking; no commissions | Only people who **signed up** to be speakers, so it misses city officials, researchers and community leaders who've never joined a speaker site; no panel builder |
| **[Speaker Discovery Engine](https://noah-cheyer.com/tools/speaker-discovery-engine)** (free custom GPT) | Suggestions with rationale from a brief | A ChatGPT wrapper: no verification described, no workflow |
| **["Speaker Finder" Claude Code skill](https://edge.blueprintgtm.com/p/speaker-finder-the-best-speaker-for)** | Rigorous funnel with sourced evidence packets per finalist | Needs a paid subscription and **5 separate API keys**; LinkedIn scraping; built for sales/marketing speakers in one city; has run once; outreach "is yours" |
| **Speaker bureaus** (BigSpeak, Leading Authorities, AllAmericanSpeakers, SPEAKING.com) | Curated, managed booking | **$7.5k+ keynotes, 20–30% commission**; professional circuit only |
| **Marketplaces / directories** ([SpeakerHub](https://speakerhub.com/), [eSpeakers](https://www.espeakers.com/), SpeakerMatch) | Free directories; calls for speakers | Self-listed speakers only; no verification beyond profiles; no panel design |
| **Event platforms** ([Sessionize](https://sessionize.com/features), [Cvent](https://cvent.com/en/event-marketing-management/content-management), Bizzabo) | Calls for papers, review, agendas, speaker portals | Manage speakers **after** they apply or are booked; they don't **discover** speakers |

### What Speaker Scout does that these don't

1. **It discovers people anywhere on the public web, not just a signed-up pool,** across **six sectors at once**: academics, practitioners, policy, founders, advocates and journalists. This reaches the city forester, the state EPR coordinator and the trade reporter, who are exactly the people niche conferences need (e.g. it found J.T. Bowers, an actual 2026 Urban Tree Summit speaker).
2. **Verification is enforced in code, not just promised.** Every link on a card must be a page actually seen during the search; each person needs a profile or work page; weak titles and LinkedIn-only matches are dropped; photos come only from the person's own profile; prompt-injection pages disqualify a candidate. General chatbots and the AI speaker tools above don't publish checks like these.
3. **Privacy by design:** it deliberately does **not** collect personal emails or phone numbers (they're scrubbed), and it **can't send email**. It drafts; humans send. Lessie's core feature is the opposite.
4. **It designs panels, not just lists.** The Panel Architect proposes cross-discipline panels with a central question, the angle each person brings, where they'd disagree, a moderator, and shared-stage history found in code. It also **reviews panels you build** and suggests who's missing. None of the products above describe this.
5. **The whole planning workflow lives in one place:** conferences → keynote and panel slots → outreach status, follow-ups, owners, outreach log, confirmed-speaker checklist, drafted emails, a research chat with sourced notes, and an at-a-glance table.
6. **Cost:** about **$0.03 per search** (measured), no API keys beyond the course key, and no subscription or commission.

### Being honest about limits

- **Lessie, SpeakUp and Speaker Finder are close cousins.** Lessie's brief-to-shortlist flow and invite drafting overlap with ours. Our difference is code-enforced verification, privacy (no contact harvesting), cross-sector coverage, panel design and review, and the planning workflow, not "AI finds speakers" by itself.
- **ChatGPT Deep Research can produce a good sourced list** if prompted carefully. It just doesn't check links and people in code, structure people by sector and speaker level, or carry the work into panels and outreach.
- **Speaker Scout only knows what's public online,** so people with little web presence (some community voices) are harder to verify. It doesn't know fees or availability, and a human still makes every decision.
